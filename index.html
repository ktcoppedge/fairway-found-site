The playbook

Leave trade times and own your tee time inventory

Many public courses trade tee times to third-party booking marketplaces in exchange for software or distribution. That can make sense, but it has a real cost. Here's the step-by-step path we use to help courses move to direct booking at a pace that fits them.

  1. 1

    Audit your current agreements

    Pull every marketplace and tee sheet contract. Note how many trade rounds you give, which days and times, renewal dates, notice periods, and any exclusivity or early-exit terms. Have your attorney review them before you make changes.

  2. 2

    Measure what trade times really cost

    Multiply trade rounds by your rack rate, then add per-round or booking fees. Use our calculator below. This number becomes your budget for doing it yourself.

  3. 3

    Set up or optimize your own booking engine

    Make sure your tee sheet's online booking is fast, mobile-first, takes payment or a card on file, and is linked from every page of your website in two taps or fewer.

  4. 4

    Turn on booking from Google

    Claim and complete your Google Business Profile, add your direct booking link, and check whether your tee sheet provider supports Reserve with Google so golfers can book from Search and Maps.

  5. 5

    Build SEO and local content

    Create pages that match how golfers search: "tee times near me," twilight rates, junior and senior rates, leagues, and nearby neighborhoods. Add golf course schema and earn reviews.

  6. 6

    Grow your email/SMS list and loyalty

    Every direct booking is a golfer you can reach again. Collect consent at booking and check-in, then send off-peak offers, weather openings, and loyalty perks.

  7. 7

    Wind down trade inventory in phases

    Within your contract terms, reduce trade rounds step by step, starting with the times your own channels already fill. Don't cut it all at once. Watch utilization and adjust.

  8. 8

    Track and report every month

    Measure direct online rounds, revenue, utilization, and cost per booking against your baseline. Move to the next phase only when the numbers support it.

Trade time cost calculator

How much are you "paying" in trade times?

Trade rounds aren't free. Each one is a tee time you could have sold at your rate. Enter your numbers below. The defaults are example values only, so replace them with yours.

Formula
Annual rounds traded = trade rounds per day × days open per year
Value given away = rounds traded × average green + cart fee
Fees paid = (rounds traded × per-round fee) + (annual online bookings × per-booking fee)
Total annual cost = value given away + fees paid

Assumes each traded round could have been sold at your average rate. Actual recovery depends on demand, so treat this as an upper-bound estimate.

    FAQ

    Common questions

    How long does SEO take to work?

    Most courses see meaningful movement in 3–6 months. Quick wins like Google profile fixes can help sooner, but search authority builds over time, which is why our performance tiers use 12-month terms.

    How does the baseline work?

    We use your tee sheet's online booking revenue for the same months in prior years. Only revenue above that baseline counts toward performance fees, and the method is written into your agreement.

    Do you work with GolfNow courses?

    Yes. Tracking options can be more limited on some marketplace-run booking systems, so we review your setup during the audit and recommend the tier that fits.

    What are the contract terms?

    Flat Fee has a 6-month minimum. Hybrid and Performance run 12 months, since SEO takes time to compound. Performance fees are capped.

    Who actually does the work?

    Our AI systems handle research, content drafts, profile updates, and reporting. Human strategists set direction, review anything public-facing, and are your point of contact.

    Leaving third-party marketplaces

    Will we lose visibility if we cut trade times?

    Marketplaces can bring in golfers who wouldn't otherwise find you, so the goal is to replace that reach before reducing it. That's why we build Google, SEO, and email first and wind down trade inventory in phases, watching utilization at each step.

    What about our current contract?

    Contracts vary. Many include renewal dates, notice periods, or minimum trade commitments. Step 1 is reviewing yours, ideally with your attorney. We plan changes within your terms and never advise breaching an agreement.

    Can we still use dynamic pricing?

    Yes. Many tee sheets support dynamic or yield pricing on your own booking engine. Owning the inventory means you set the rules: off-peak discounts, twilight rates, and peak premiums, without trading the rounds away.

    How long does the transition take?

    It depends on your contract and demand. A common pattern is 1–2 months to set up direct booking and Google, then a phased reduction of trade rounds over a season or more. We move to each phase only when your numbers support it.

    Do we have to leave marketplaces completely?

    No. Some courses keep a small amount of marketplace inventory for reach. The point is to make it a choice based on real numbers, not a default.
